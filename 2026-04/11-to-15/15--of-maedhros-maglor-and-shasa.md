The Weight of the Oath
----------------------

Maedhros and Maglor were the primary leaders of the Fëanorean faction in Middle-earth. Along with their father and five brothers, they swore the **Oath of Fëanor**: a vow to recover the three Silmarils (holy jewels) at any cost, calling down the "Everlasting Darkness" upon themselves if they failed.

*   **The Reluctant Warrior:** While he fought in every major battle, Maglor is often portrayed as the brother with the most active conscience, frequently questioning the path their oath forced them to take.

## Prompt:

## Response:
To understand why Maedhros relinquishing the High Kingship was such a monumental event, you have to look at the intersection of Elven law, family pride, and the desperate need for political unity.

In the eyes of many, this was Maedhros’s finest moment—a selfless act that likely delayed the total destruction of the Noldor by centuries.

The Line of Succession
----------------------

The High Kingship of the Noldor originally belonged to **Finwë**. After Finwë was slain by Morgoth, the title passed to his eldest son, **Fëanor**. When Fëanor died shortly after arriving in Middle-earth, **Maedhros**, as his eldest son, was technically the King of all the Noldor.

However, there was a massive rift in the family. Fëanor had famously betrayed his half-brothers, **Fingolfin** and **Finarfin**, by stealing the ships at Alqualondë and leaving them to cross the grinding ice of the Helcaraxë on foot. Many of the Noldor who survived that crossing blamed the House of Fëanor for their suffering and deaths.

The Transfer of Power
---------------------

After Fingon rescued Maedhros from the peaks of Thangorodrim, Maedhros realized that if he claimed the crown, the Noldor would remain divided and Morgoth would easily crush them.

To heal the feud, Maedhros begged forgiveness for the desertion at the ships and **waived his claim to the kingship**. He passed the title to his uncle, **Fingolfin**, for two main reasons:

1.  **Seniority:** Fingolfin was the eldest surviving son of Finwë.
2.  **Popularity:** The vast majority of the Noldor followed Fingolfin and held no love for Fëanor’s sons.

### The Political Fallout

While this act brought a period of peace (the "Leaguer of Angband"), it didn't sit well with everyone:

*   **The Brothers:** Maedhros’s younger brothers (particularly the hot-headed Curufin and Celegorm) were "not all of one mind" with this decision. They felt the House of Fëanor was being robbed of its birthright.
*   **The "Dispossessed":** Because of this event, the House of Fëanor became known as **the Dispossessed**, a title that carried a heavy sting of lost pride and fueled future tensions.

Impact on the Noldorin Hierarchy
--------------------------------

By stepping down, Maedhros fundamentally changed the map of Beleriand. The House of Fingolfin ruled from **Hithlum**, while Maedhros moved his brothers to the **March of Maedhros** in the east.

This move was strategic: Maedhros placed himself and his brothers in the most dangerous, exposed lands, acting as a "buffer zone" against Morgoth to prove his loyalty to the new High King.

### Successive High Kings after the Transfer

Because Maedhros stepped aside, the crown followed Fingolfin’s line until the end of the First Age:

1.  **Fingolfin** (Maedhros's Uncle)
2.  **Fingon** (Maedhros's Cousin and best friend)
3.  **Turgon** (Fingon's Brother)
4.  **Gil-galad** (Fingon's Son/Grandson, depending on the version of the text)

Had Maedhros kept the crown, the internal politics of Middle-earth might have been even bloodier, as the other houses likely would have refused to follow a King bound to the violent Oath of Fëanor.

## Prompt:

## Response:
The parentage of **Ereinon Gil-galad** is one of the most famous "moving targets" in Tolkien’s legendarium. If you feel confused about who his father actually was, you’re in good company—Tolkien himself changed his mind multiple times over several decades.

The Two Main Versions
---------------------

Depending on which book you pick up, Gil-galad has a different father.

### 1\. The Published _Silmarillion_ Version

In the version edited by Christopher Tolkien and Guy Kay, Gil-galad is the **son of Fingon**.

*   **The Logic:** This makes him the direct heir to the High Kingship after Fingon falls in the _Nirnaeth Arnoediad_.
*   **The Story:** As a child, he is sent to the Havens of the Falas for safety during the wars, eventually becoming the last High King of the Noldor in Middle-earth.

### 2\. The _History of Middle-earth_ Version (The "Final" Intent)

Later, while examining his father’s deeper notes for _The Peoples of Middle-earth_, Christopher Tolkien realized his father had moved away from the Fingon idea. In this later version, Gil-galad is the **son of Orodreth**.

*   **The Genealogy:** Orodreth (who in this version is the son of Angrod, not Finarfin) makes Gil-galad a member of the **House of Finarfin**.
*   **The Conflict:** Christopher Tolkien later admitted that making him Fingon’s son in the published _Silmarillion_ was likely an editorial error based on an older, discarded manuscript.

Who was he, really?
-------------------

Regardless of his father, Gil-galad’s legacy is defined by his long reign in the **Second Age**. While Maedhros and Maglor were defined by a tragic oath, Gil-galad was defined by his steadfastness against Sauron.

### Key Facts:

*   **The Name:** _Ereinon_ was his birth name (meaning "Scion of Kings"), while _Gil-galad_ was his mother-name/epessë, meaning **"Star of Radiance."**
*   **The Kingdom:** He ruled from **Lindon**, the coastal land west of the Blue Mountains that survived the sinking of Beleriand.
*   **The Alliance:** He formed the **Last Alliance of Elves and Men** with Elendil.
*   **The Weapon:** He famously wielded the spear **Aeglos** ("Snow-point"), which no Orc could withstand.

The Connection to Maedhros & Maglor
-----------------------------------

There is a poetic irony in Gil-galad’s reign. He was the High King that **Elrond** served as herald.

Think about the circle:

1.  Maedhros gave up the crown to the House of Fingolfin (Gil-galad's line in the published version).
2.  Maglor and Maedhros raised Elrond.
3.  Elrond then became the chief counselor and right hand to the king who held the crown the sons of Fëanor had "lost."

### His Death

Gil-galad’s death marked the end of the High Kingship of the Noldor in Middle-earth. He died during the Siege of Barad-dûr, killed by the heat of **Sauron’s own hand**. Because he left no heir, the title died with him, and the Elves of the Third Age (like those in Rivendell and Lothlórien) were ruled by Lords and Ladies rather than a High King.

Eärendil the Mariner
--------------------

Eärendil was the son of Tuor and Idril—a Half-elven child of immense destiny. He grew up at the Mouths of Sirion, where he eventually married **Elwing** (the granddaughter of Beren and Lúthien).

With the world falling to Morgoth, Eärendil built the ship **Vingilot** ("Foam-flower") to sail into the West. He sought to find Valinor and beg the Valar for aid. For years he failed, until Elwing brought him the **Silmaril** that her parents had recovered.

*   **Eärendil** chose to be an Elf for the sake of Elwing.
